Tool 02

How money gets in — and back out again

The eCCI is the single most consequential document in Nigerian foreign investment, and the one most often skipped for speed. Step through it.

Capital pathway Step 1 of 5
1

Foreign capital leaves your account

Equity, shareholder loan or acquisition consideration. It must travel in the company's name, through formal banking channels.

Using informal FX channels because they are faster or cheaper is the decision that makes everything downstream unfixable.
2

It arrives at an authorised dealer bank in Nigeria

Not every bank handles capital importation competently. Choosing one with genuine eCCI experience is worth more than a marginally better FX rate.

3

The bank documents the inflow

Source, purpose, beneficiary company and instrument type are recorded and reported to the Central Bank.

4

The eCCI is issued — within 24 hours

The electronic Certificate of Capital Importation is your legal proof that this money entered Nigeria as foreign investment. Collect it on every single tranche, not just the first.

No eCCI, no clean repatriation. This is not always fixable retroactively, and it is the most common way foreign money becomes trapped in Nigeria.
5

Profits, dividends and capital can be repatriated

With eCCIs in hand and taxes settled, your bank can source FX and remit dividends, loan repayments or sale proceeds through official channels at the prevailing rate.

The one-sentence version. Paperwork at money-in protects money-out. The eCCI is the difference between profits you can repatriate and profits that are stuck in Lagos.

Where people go wrong. Capital arrives before the company exists. Or it lands in a director's personal account "to save time". Or the first tranche gets an eCCI and the next four do not. Each of those is recoverable only partially, and only sometimes.
